# Forensic audit — Bank of England 25%, Tesla 50%, OPEC+ 100%

Every figure below is recomputed from the runs' own artifacts by
`scripts/forensics.py`, cross-checked by two independent read-only auditors that were
given the artifact paths and nothing else. Per-run evidence:
`artifacts/forensics/<run>/` (13 artifacts each, including `run_dossier.html`).

**All three probabilities reconstruct exactly. Two of the three are arithmetic over
equal, ungrounded weights, and should not be described as products of social
simulation.**

## The ten questions, answered plainly

**1. Why was Bank of England exactly 25%?**
Because one of four equally weighted branches resolved YES. Two binary uncertainties
(`job_market_condition`, `inflation_outlook`) enumerate a 2×2 grid; each cell weighs
0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25. `PERFECT_FRACTION_FROM_BRANCH_WEIGHTS`.

**2. Was it one YES branch out of four equally weighted branches?**
Yes. Exactly: `NO, NO, YES, NO` at 0.25 each.

**3. Were those branch weights evidence-grounded or symmetric ignorance?**
Symmetric ignorance, and not even model-chosen. Every alternative in the accepted plan
carried `"weight": null`; `semantic_lowering.py` then minted `weight = 1.0/n` and
stamped `symmetric_ignorance_assumption`. All four outcomes carry that label; no branch
is `weight_grounded`. One of the two values on the decisive variable is the filler
label `"other"`, whose own grounding string reads *"No specific alternative in
evidence."* — and that cell carries the entire YES mass.

**4. Did any actor decision change a branch from YES to NO or NO to YES?**
Yes. 14 actor invocations across the four branches. The actor executed
`signal_support_for_cut` only in the `slowing ∧ other` branch and waited in the other
three. The terminal is an event count (`event_count('bailey_s_public_signal_of_support')
> 0`), and both such events carry `actor_id: andrew_bailey`.

**5. Would BoE remain 25% if all actor outputs were replaced with no-op?**
No. It becomes **0.0**. Deleting actor-produced events removes the only events of the
counted type, so every branch resolves NO. The trajectory is *necessary* for any YES
at all — but it does not set the magnitude.

**6. Why was Tesla exactly 50%?**
Because one of two equally weighted branches resolved YES. A single uncertainty with
two alternatives — again `weight: null` → `1.0/n` = 0.5 each.

**7. Was it one positive and one negative branch with equal starting weight?**
Yes: factor 0.8 → NO, factor 1.05 → YES, 0.5 each.

**8. Were the production quantities generated by operating processes or preloaded by
branch assumptions?**
**Preloaded, then multiplied.** `q3_deliveries = q2_deliveries × seasonal_factor`, where
`q2_deliveries = 480,126` is cited (`c-82fa536178c2`) and the factor is the branch's own
assumption. 480,126 × 0.8 = 384,100.8 (NO); × 1.05 = 504,132.3 (YES). The break-even
factor is 400,000 / 480,126 = 0.8331. **The two invented factors straddle it**, so the
choice of those two numbers, and the fact that there are two of them, fixes the answer
entirely. There is no operating process: zero actor invocations, one scheduling batch
per branch, one `set_field` each. Had the planner written 0.9 instead of 0.8, the
published number would be 1.00 with nothing else changed.

**9. Would Tesla remain 50% if actor outputs were removed?**
Yes — unchanged, because there were **no actor outputs to remove**. Removing the
non-actor process instead yields UNRESOLVED in both branches (the unset-quantity guard
fires). So the single multiplication is what converts "unknown" into "50%".

**10. Did any simulation behavior change the final probability, or did the initial
world weights already determine it?**

- **BoE**: simulation determined *which cells* are YES (a conjunction: YES ⟺ slowing ∧
  other); the equal ungrounded weights determined *the number*. Re-weight the same
  cells to any evidence-plausible asymmetry and the probability moves with the
  trajectory untouched.
- **Tesla**: simulation contributed one multiplication. Nothing decided anything.
- **OPEC+**: nothing ran at all — zero batches, zero events, zero actor invocations. The
  terminal field `increase_announced` was `True` at t₀ from a cited pre-cutoff
  announcement (`c-1e7c9d751ad5`, published 2026-07-05, twenty days before the cutoff)
  and no event ever wrote it. Removing every recorded event leaves the answer YES.

## Stated plainly, as required

**The BoE and Tesla results are the weighted count of equally weighted assumptions.**
Neither may be called calibrated or simulation-derived. Both runs' own machinery says
so already: `probability_source = scenario_enumeration_ungrounded_weights`,
`point_estimate_is_calibrated = false`, bounds `[0.0, 1.0]` — the widest possible
interval, which is the system stating that its own point estimate constrains nothing.

**The OPEC+ 1.00 is a factual resolution, not a forecast.** The record answered the
question before the window opened. That is a legitimate outcome — but the run published
it under `probability_source = "weighted_simulated_trajectories"` with bounds
`[1.0, 1.0]`, which claims a provenance the trace cannot support. **Fixed**: such a run
now reports `established_before_simulation_from_cited_record`.

One epistemic caveat on OPEC+ that no code check caught: the store contains two claims
stating group-wide quotas were held unchanged through 2026-12-31, while the cited
increases are seven-country voluntary-cut unwindings agreed at sub-group meetings.
Under the reading "will the ministerial process raise group-wide quotas," the same
record supports NO. The run resolved that ambiguity silently and published 1.00 with
bounds [1.0, 1.0]. The answer is defensible on the plain reading; the *certainty* is not.

## Should these results be trusted?

- **OPEC+ 1.00** — trust the *answer* on the plain reading of the question; do not trust
  the `[1.0, 1.0]` bounds, and do not call it a simulation result. It is the record.
- **BoE 0.25** — trust the *structure* (the conjunction is evidence-linked and the actor
  genuinely produced it); do not trust the *number*. It is one of four arbitrarily
  weighted scenarios, and the cell carrying all the YES mass rests on an alternative
  the planner itself could not name.
- **Tesla 0.50** — do not trust it as a forecast. It is `1/n` over two invented factors
  that straddle the threshold, with no actor, no decision and no operating process.

All three runs remain valid as *traces*. Two of the three are not valid as *forecasts
produced by social simulation*, and this repository should not describe them that way.
